# Content Performance Analysis: The Humans of BRACU

## Tools Used
- Python (Pandas,Colab)
- Microsoft Excel
- Data Visualization Libraries

## Business Problem
Content creators often struggle to identify why certain posts achieve viral reach while high-production posts underperform. This analysis evaluates performance patterns across "The Humans of BRACU" campaign to uncover narrative triggers that maximize engagement and drive organic follower growth.

## The Data
- **Source:** Meta Content Performance dataset covering June 09, 2026 – September 07, 2026.
- **Dimensions:** 18 posts (rows) and 20 performance variables (columns).
- **Key Variables:** Views, Impressions, Net Follows, Interactions (Reactions, Shares, Comments), and Post Titles.

## Methodology
- **Data Cleaning:** Parsed datetime objects, filtered non-content metadata rows, and structured post transcript titles[cite: 1].
- **Metric Engineering:** Calculated viewer engagement rates and follower conversion efficiency ratios[cite: 1].
- **Exploratory Analysis:** Categorized post content into narrative archetypes to identify psychological performance triggers.

## Key Findings & Insights
- **Vulnerability Drives Virality:** Posts featuring raw personal struggle (e.g., comeback from a 2.26 CGPA) achieved peak reach with 131,418 views and 316 net follows.
- **Subverting Stereotypes:** Unconventional faculty spotlights ("paid to yap") generated high engagement with 76,257 views and 182 net follows.
- **The "Success Alienation" Trap:** Posts heavily emphasizing commercial deals and achievements underperformed (13,502 views) due to a lack of relatable central conflict.

## Recommendations
- **Lead with Failure:** Structure stories around personal or academic setbacks before revealing success.
- **Humanize Authority Figures:** Highlight informal, creative side-passions when featuring faculty members.
- **Prioritize Process over PR:** Focus on personal growth and mentorship rather than corporate resume accomplishments.

## Limitations & Challenges
This analysis is limited by a small sample size of 18 posts over a 90-day period, subjective qualitative categorizations of post titles, and external social media platform algorithm fluctuations.
